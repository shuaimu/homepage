Follows ../notes/spanner-sql.md section by section and Bacon, Bales, Bruno, Cooper, Dickinson, Fikes, Fraser, Gubarev, Joshi, Kogan, Lloyd, Melnik, Rao, Shue, Taylor, van der Holst, and Woodford, Spanner: Becoming a SQL System (SIGMOD 2017, pages 331–343). Route and timing

The deck has two parts: a main route sized for about 44 slides, and five backup slides.

Slides 1–44 are the main route, ending on the recap, slide 44. They keep the four mechanisms the paper is about and the setup they need: the machine and interleaving (6–7), Q1 and the two strawmen (9–11), distribution as an operator with partitionability, joins written as Apply (18) and the walk that distributes Q1, then shard pruning (13–22), batched distributed joins (24–27), range extraction from the start/stop-key strawman to one scan per key column (29–31), and restarts up to resharding (33–38), then, after a “Spanner Lessons” divider (39), four lessons from production, a figure each: users wanted SQL, plans must not regress, scale first, then go relational, and SQL vs. NoSQL with one size fits all (40–43). The speaker-note timings total 61 minutes; they assume a brisk pace, so a slower one, with questions, fills an 80-minute slot.

Slide 45 opens the backup: five self-contained slides, about a minute each, for when the main route finishes early, usable in any order: one call per Paxos group and shard affinity (46), Q2’s two-level plan (47), location hints (48), parallel consumers (49), and locking rows that are not there yet, a preview of Nov 11 (50).

The backup’s speaker-note timings total 5 minutes. The handout carries both parts. Earlier versions also had an appendix: the slides cut from the main route (shards moving, Q1 on one machine, how Scan2 skips, one predicate’s three uses, the filter tree and the slides that prepare it, why extraction is safe, tokens across reruns and releases, what restarts bought, why inside, not beside), the pull-up rule cards, the notes’ optional parts (one SQL dialect, Ressi, the paper’s full lessons), the design assembled, where the idea went and the review questions. It was removed before publishing; the lecture notes cover all of it.

The main route covers what the cut slides used to show in two ways. Slide 6’s speaker note says aloud that shards split, merge and move between groups at any time, which strawman 2 (slide 11) needs. Q1’s plan tree first appears as the start of the walk, slide 20, and strawman 1 (slide 10) speaks of running “all of Q1” rather than its tree. Otherwise it claims nothing that only the backup shows: the recap (44) shows six topics with a few reminder words each, no sentences, and its note says the line behind each, with Paxos-group calls and affinity left to backup slide 46. The notes of slides 22, 28 and 44 point to the lecture notes for one predicate’s three uses, predicates with OR and NOT, and why extracted ranges may be too big but never too small, and the recap’s note points to the review questions at the end of the lecture notes.

If time is shorter still, skip slides 2 (why Spanner became SQL), 14 (pull the union up) and 38 (resharding) first. Never skip slides 15–16 and 20, partitionability and the walk that distributes Q1; slide 27, where batching makes the index pay per shard touched; slide 31, one scan per key column; or slides 35–36, the restart token and its timestamp.

The locked scenario

The shard map, the arrays, the Denver batch, the version counts and the restart traces are illustrative; the paper gives none. The paper’s own examples are its Figure 1 query (Q1 here), the Documents table of section 4 with its parameters P001 and 2017-01-01 and its ten documents under /proposals, the filter-tree predicate of Figure 4, and the resharding example of section 5 ([20, 30) merged into [10, 40)); the deck uses all but the filter tree, which is in the notes only, says in slide 29’s note which Documents values are the paper’s, names the documents doc1 to doc10 (so the path after doc1 is doc10, not the paper’s doc2), and adds the following, consistent with the notes:

  • Customer is sharded on ckey into S1 [−∞, 250) in G1, S2 [250, 500) in G2, S3 [500, 750) in G1 and S4 [750, +∞) in G3; Sales is interleaved in Customer. G1 holding two shards is what slide 46’s “four shards, three calls” relies on. Slide 6 draws the map and its note asks the class to photograph it.
  • Q1’s array is [7, 412, 7, 950]: deduplicated to 7, 412 and 950, it calls S1, S2 and S4 and never S3 (slide 22).
  • Q2 (c.city = 'Denver') uses the global index CustomerByCity, key (city, ckey), sharded on city at Chicago and Miami, so index shard I2 holds all of Denver. Denver’s entries are ckeys 7, 300, 310, 820 and 990: five rows in three Customer shards, five calls one after another without batching (slide 25), three calls in parallel with it (slide 27). Slide 25’s note uses 1,000 Denver customers for the row-at-a-time cost.
  • Each of the paper’s ten documents gets 1,000 versions: one start/stop range reads 10,000 rows to keep 10 (slide 30), and the correlated scans of slide 31 seek each document’s one wanted version instead. Paths are strings, so in key order /proposals/doc10 comes right after /proposals/doc1: slide 30’s strip shows those two and draws doc2 to doc9 as one box.
  • The restart scenario is one query over 50 shards streaming a million rows, 400,000 consumed (slide 33). The timestamp figure moves one row between keys 10 and 90 at ts 110, between halves read at ts 100 and ts 120 (slide 36). The resharding trace on slide 38 uses the paper’s ranges with our timestamp, keys and servers.

Statements the notes mark as “our reading” stay marked in the speaker notes; on screen they are stated plainly, like everything else, since no slide refers to the paper: hash sharding sufficing for GroupBy(ckey) (slide 16), the Apply pull-up rule, which slide 47’s figure states beside Q2’s two-level plan, and why the key-column lock covers a range (slide 50).

The deck as a whole and the notes teach the same content in the same order, and nothing on the slides goes beyond the notes except the lesson slides (40–43): their Cloud-customer caption, the six-step path with its advice arrow, the regression curves, and slide 43’s burden of running several systems and its goal of doing well across that range come from the paper’s sections 8 and 9, beyond the notes’ shorter lessons list. The main route presents a subset of the notes and the backup slides a little more; the rest, including “The rules, collected” and “Questions to review”, is in the notes only. The paper’s two slips (Timestamp once for Version, DocumentId in Figure 3) are mentioned only in slide 29’s note.

Standards

The deck follows the query-optimization deck’s standard. Every slide stands alone in the handout: its claim is argued on the slide, and no technical word appears on screen before a slide has defined it. No slide cites another slide by number on screen; the cross-references live in the speaker notes and this README. Nor does any slide after the title refer to the paper: no “the paper says”, no quoted sentences, no “ours” against “the paper’s”. Each slide states its content directly, and the speaker notes keep the attributions and section numbers. On the main route, terms are defined where first used: shard on slide 3 (as key ranges) and with the Paxos group on slide 6, Bigtable and OLTP on slide 2, TiKV on slide 4, sharding key on slide 7, the array @arr on slide 9 (slide 10 says “the array”, not “IN list”), CrossApply and OuterApply on slide 18, Top(5) on slide 16, DistributedUnion and the root server on slide 13, partitionability and OrderedUnionAll on slide 15, OpLocal and OpFinal on slide 17, the sharding-key filter on slide 20, shard pruning on slide 22, DistributedApply, with Batch and Unnest, drawn on slide 26 (its caption was dropped), the restart token and the snapshot timestamp on slide 35, t_safe on slide 36, strong reads, the leader replica and wound-wait on slide 37. Slide 3 says “a query at one timestamp” rather than “snapshot”. The backup slides define their own terms: shard affinity (46), the location hint (48), root-partitionable and parallel consumers (49), and phantom (50). Slide 30 says “test key columns” rather than last lecture’s “factors match”, slide 18 avoids “column op value”, slide 24 writes Q2 as a join over the index table rather than naming an access path, and slide 25 labels the nested-loop cost formula and names its outer and inner, so no slide needs the previous lecture’s vocabulary to be read. The speaker notes follow the same rule for what the lecturer says aloud: a term from Oct 19 or last lecture gets a phrase of gloss where it first comes up (wound-wait, t_safe, interesting order, Table 1’s selectivity guesses, PD, a TiFlash learner).

The look is the query-optimization deck’s: spannersql-theme.typ is a copy of queryopt-theme.typ with this lecture’s title, date and DOI, plus size and inset options on the comparison table helper. Serif titles, white pages, thin grey rules, red for the one operator, shard, call or range under discussion. Body text is 24pt with 1.5× baseline spacing and tables 21pt; dense slides were reflowed or split rather than set smaller. Code blocks use the sql helper, whose size argument is about 1.47 times the rendered size because the theme’s raw-text rules compound; the sizes in the source were picked by rendering.

spannersql-diagrams.typ holds 27 editable CeTZ figures and no images; three of them, key-range-call, filter-tree and pax-block, drew the removed appendix and are unused, kept so those slides can be restored. It copies the query-optimization deck’s drawing primitives rather than importing them, so the two decks can change independently. Every figure that draws Customer’s shard strip uses the same geometry (shard-row), so S1–S4 sit in the same place on slides 6, 10, 22, 24, 25 and 27; slide 49 instead groups the four shards under its two workers. Slides 24, 25 and 27 also open with the same one-line lead, so the index figure does not move between them. The four lesson figures, sql-growth, plan-regression, sql-path and nosql-stack, draw slides 40–43 in one state each; the second’s curves are illustrative, as its caption says. Twelve figures in the deck take a stage:, step: or mode: parameter, and several keep identical coordinates across states so the picture stays still while one thing changes: q1-tree draws the six states of slide 20 and slide 21 ("final"), with only the union, and from step 1 its sharding-key filter, moving; index-calls draws slides 24, 25 and 27 ("index" then "scatter", "row", "batch"); doc-keys draws slide 30 ("range"; its "seek" mode drew a removed slide); coupling draws slide 4, the loose design alone and then both; du-fanout draws slide 13, the compiled tree alone and then the run-time calls too (slide 14’s pull-up, which takes no parameter, redraws that tree with a “pull up” arrow and the root and shard brackets); parent-child-merge draws slide 19’s three states, the interleaved shards, then the plan before, then after the two unions merge; self-join-tree draws the six states of slide 31, whose last state, the handout page, is the tree with a one-line summary; beside, pruning, long-query, resume-ts and reshard-trace draw the reveals of slides 10, 22, 33, 36 and 38. Every reveal only adds to the picture except on slides 10, 20 and 31: on 10 the second state replaces the with-array picture (S3 greyed, thin arrows) with the no-array one, its caption keeping its baseline; on 20 the union moves up the tree and the panel reddens the current step; on 31 the tree is built bottom up, Scan1, Scan2, their CrossApply, Scan3, the top CrossApply, each red with its edges as it appears, with a line naming what it needs or feeds, and turns black on the next click. Multi-line text inside figures, prose and the scan boxes’ code alike, goes through the spaced helper, which gives it the body text’s 1.5× baseline spacing (CeTZ labels do not inherit the theme’s text edges); no figure text is smaller than 13pt. The last state of each slide is its handout page.

The whole deck, every reveal state included, and the handout were rendered and inspected page by page at 960 × 540. An independent review then checked the slides and notes against the paper and the notes, the teaching standard, the figures and every rendered page; 70 findings survived a second reviewer’s attempt to refute them and were applied, the deck was rendered and inspected again, and a second, smaller review of the fixes found 27 more, mostly line wraps and README details, which were applied the same way. The deck was then cut to a 45-slide main route at the lecturer’s request, with five backup slides and the rest moved to the appendix; every speaker-note cross-reference was renumbered, the transitions around the cut slides were rewritten, and every page was inspected again. A term audit followed: four reviewers, three by slide range and one by term, listed every word used on screen or in a note before the deck defined it, and three skeptics tried to refute each; 80 survived (many duplicates). Thirty-eight on-screen edits and thirty-two note glosses were applied, every changed slide was re-rendered in each state, and seven that then wrapped badly or crowded the footer were reworded to fit. Slide 18 was also reworded so that Apply differs from last lecture’s nested loop in what its inner side can be, not in whether the inner side sees the outer row. The lecturer then moved the three slides that keep the AND/OR tree, build the filter tree and argue why extraction is safe into the appendix, beside the two that prepare the leaves, which shortened the main route to 42 slides and 58 minutes. A script renumbered every note cross-reference and each was checked against its target’s title, and the recap’s ranges line was rewritten so that the main route claims nothing it no longer shows. Three lesson slides with figures were then added before the recap (40–42), which moved the recap after them and the main route to 61 minutes; the appendix’s text lists of lessons (70–71) stay as the full version. Then slides 2 and 3 were swapped, so the reason Spanner became SQL comes before the bridge from System R, and slides 2, 3 and 4 got reveals (62 minutes); the dividers on slides 5 and 8 were retitled “Revisit Spanner” and “Example Query”. Later, one predicate’s three uses moved to the appendix (55), ahead of the filter-tree slides, and why inside, not beside followed it (63), after what restarts bought; the main route is now 43 slides, 60 minutes. A “Spanner Lessons” divider (39) then went in before the lessons. Joins as Apply moved from right after Q1 to just before the first join the union meets (18), so the strawmen follow Q1 directly. A fourth lesson, SQL vs. NoSQL and one size fits all (43), went in before the recap, which now shows its six topics as reminder words. Scan2 skips then moved to the appendix too, so the main route is 44 slides, the recap at 44. Before publishing, the appendix was removed from the deck (50 slides); its notes references now point to the lecture notes. Four of the first round’s fixes also corrected the lecture notes, which the deck follows: the point-lookup hop (one call becomes two, not “most of the latency”), the doc10 key order, the NOT push-down example, and a floating-point SUM’s input order changing its value rather than the row order.

Requires Typst 0.15 or later (checked with 0.15.1). Imports pin Touying 0.7.4 and CeTZ 0.5.2.

make -C teaching/db/26fa/slides spannersql
make -C teaching/db/26fa/slides spannersql-export   # the HTML player

typst compile DB26fa-SpannerSQL.typ
typst compile --input handout=true DB26fa-SpannerSQL.typ DB26fa-SpannerSQL-handout.pdf
typst eval --in DB26fa-SpannerSQL.typ --pretty 'query(<pdfpc-file>).first().value' > DB26fa-SpannerSQL.pdfpc
